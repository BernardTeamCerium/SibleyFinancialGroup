Our services

Retirement planning, handled as one coordinated whole

Income, growth, protection, taxes and legacy are not six separate problems. They pull on each other, and planning them in isolation is how good savers end up with disappointing retirements.

01 — Turning savings into a paycheck

Retirement Income Planning

The day you retire, your money changes jobs. It stops growing a balance and starts paying a salary — and almost nothing about accumulating wealth prepares a portfolio for that.

We build the income side of your retirement first: what arrives each month, where it comes from, which portion is guaranteed, and how it keeps pace as prices rise over a retirement that may run thirty years.

That means coordinating every source you have — Social Security, pensions, 401(k) and IRA balances, annuity income, rental or business income — into a single schedule rather than a pile of statements. It also means deciding the order you draw from them, which is one of the few retirement decisions that can add years to how long the money lasts without adding a dollar of risk.

401(k) & IRA rollovers

Consolidating old employer plans into a strategy that pays you, with the tax consequences handled correctly.

Guaranteed income floor

Covering your essential monthly expenses with income that continues no matter what markets do.

Withdrawal sequencing

Deciding which account to draw from each year so taxes and longevity risk both stay under control.

02 — Market-linked growth, protected principal

Safe Growth Strategies

Safe growth tied to the market is the discipline this firm is known for: participating when markets rise, without handing back those gains when markets fall.

Most people approaching retirement are caught between two bad options — take full market risk with money they will need in five years, or park it somewhere that loses to inflation quietly every year. Neither is a plan.

Fixed indexed annuities and similar principal-protected vehicles occupy the ground between. Your account is credited based on the performance of a market index, subject to caps, spreads or participation rates, but it does not lose value because of index declines. We explain exactly how the crediting works, what the trade-offs are, what you give up in exchange for the protection, and where the surrender period leaves you — before anything is signed.

Principal protection

Index-linked crediting with contractual protection against loss from market downturns.

Sequence-of-returns risk

Shielding the first decade of retirement, when a bad market does the most permanent damage.

Full disclosure of trade-offs

Caps, participation rates, fees, riders and surrender schedules explained in writing, in advance.

03 — Protecting what is already built

Wealth Preservation & Risk Management

After a certain point, the priority stops being accumulation and becomes protection. Losing 30% is no longer an inconvenience to ride out — it is a change to how you will live.

We review the whole picture for the risks that quietly erode retirement wealth: excessive portfolio risk carried out of habit, fees compounding in the background, inflation on fixed income, and the cost of an extended care event that can consume a lifetime of savings in a few years.

The work is unglamorous and it is where most of the value sits. Frequently the recommendation is to take risk off the table, simplify what you hold, and make sure the surviving spouse would be fine if something happened tomorrow.

Portfolio risk review

An honest assessment of how much market exposure your plan actually requires you to carry.

Long-term care exposure

Strategies to fund extended care without liquidating the estate to do it.

Inflation & fee drag

Finding the costs and erosion working against you, and removing what does not earn its keep.

04 — Keeping more of what you earned

Tax-Efficient & Social Security Strategy

Two retirees with identical savings can end up with materially different spendable income, purely from the order they withdraw and the year they claim Social Security.

We coordinate the tax side of retirement: the balance between taxable, tax-deferred and tax-free accounts, whether Roth conversions make sense in your lower-income years, how required minimum distributions will land, and how to avoid the avoidable — the Social Security tax torpedo, an IRMAA bracket crossed for the sake of a few hundred dollars.

Your Social Security claiming date is part of the same decision. Claiming early, at full retirement age, or at seventy is not a question with one right answer; it depends on your health, your spouse, your other income and your tax position. We model the options with your actual numbers so you can see the difference rather than guess at it.

Social Security timing

Modelling claiming ages for you and your spouse, including survivor benefits.

Roth conversion windows

Identifying the years between retiring and RMDs where conversions may pay for themselves.

Medicare & IRMAA awareness

Planning income so premium surcharges are not triggered by accident.

05 — Passing it on cleanly

Estate & Legacy Planning

A legacy is not only what you leave. It is how easily it reaches the people you intended, and how much of it survives the journey.

We work alongside your attorney and CPA to make sure the financial side of your estate is in order: beneficiary designations current and correctly titled, accounts structured so they pass outside probate where appropriate, and your wishes documented rather than assumed.

It is remarkably common to find a policy still naming a former spouse, or an IRA whose beneficiary form was never updated after a child was born. These documents override a will. Reviewing them is an hour of work that has saved families years of conflict.

Beneficiary review

Auditing every account and policy so nothing passes to the wrong person by default.

Probate avoidance

Titling and designation strategies that keep assets out of a public, slow process.

Coordination with counsel

Working with your attorney and accountant so the plan and the documents agree.

06 — Protection sized to the people who depend on you

Life Insurance & Final Expense

Life insurance is either the most over-sold product in this industry or the most necessary, depending entirely on whether the amount and the type match the obligation.

We start from the obligation: what would actually need to be replaced, for how long, and what is already covered. Sometimes that points to term coverage during the working years. Sometimes it points to permanent coverage with living benefits for chronic or critical illness. Often, for a client whose children are grown and whose mortgage is paid, it points to less coverage than they are currently carrying.

For older clients, final expense coverage serves a narrower but real purpose: making sure a funeral is not paid for out of a grieving family’s savings, or out of a credit card.

Needs-based analysis

A calculated coverage amount, not a number produced by a sales target.

Policy review

Examining what you already own — performance, cost and whether it still fits.

Final expense

Modest, straightforward coverage that keeps a funeral from becoming a financial event.
